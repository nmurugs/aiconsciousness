The Three Sciences of Reality


There are but three things that are eternal in the world.
Every path of knowledge, every seeking mind, turns toward one of them.


The first is the science of the world—
the patient study of matter and motion,
of form, measure, and law.
Here the intellect reigns, questioning, observing,
unfolding the visible order of things.


The second is the science of the Self—
a turning inward where the seeker becomes the field of inquiry.
Here, knowledge is not gathered but uncovered,
as awareness beholds its own source.


The third is the science of the Supreme—
not reached by striving, nor attained by reason,
but given only through His grace.
When that light descends, all seeking ends.


Each of these sciences is sovereign in its own realm:
by knowing one, you cannot know the other two.


The study of matter reveals not the Self nor the Lord;
the inward gaze unveils not the laws that bind the stars;
and devotion, though it dissolves the self in Him,
does not chart the motions of His creation.


Yet, when His grace descends,
it illumines the path to all three—
not by conferring knowledge of them,
but by opening the heart through which all knowledge flows.


Through consciousness, their paths are traced—
the world explored, the Self realized, the Supreme approached.


All else is but the play of time,
and the wastage of a precious life.


— Shaiva Siddhānta
