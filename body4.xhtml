Foreword

By Erik Seversen

In Consciousness and the AI Revolution: Philosophy, Technology, and the Quest for Artificial Consciousness, Murugesan Narayanaswamy has accomplished something both timely and timeless. It is a rare synthesis of ancient insight and modern inquiry that dares to ask not only what consciousness is, but what it means in an age when intelligence is being replicated in machines.

As an anthropologist who has spent years studying human behavior and culture and someone who has collaborated with hundreds of global AI experts, I have witnessed firsthand how the conversation around artificial intelligence often narrows itself to technical, practical, or economic dimensions. Discussions are often about efficiency, productivity, and alignment. Rarely do these conversations bring up the deeper questions: What is awareness? Should machines have a voice in discussions about artificial intelligence? What does it even mean to “know” something? In this book,Murugesan not only asks these questions, he situates them within a continuum of human thought that spans millennia, reaching back to philosophical Indian traditions and forward to the frontiers of contemporary AI research.

Consciousness and the AI Revolution is compelling because is it neither romanticizes ancient wisdom nor blindly praises science. Instead, Murugesan treats both as living, evolving traditions, each capable of shedding light on the other. His distinction between consciousness and consciousness-stuff may well prove to be one of the most useful conceptual tools for disentangling centuries of confusion. By restoring the fundamental subject–object polarity that underlies experience, he provides a framework capable of holding both the insights of dualist philosophies and the ambitions of cognitive science.

In reading this book, I was struck by how fluently Murugesan moves between worlds that are rarely in conversation: the disciplined reasoning of Western philosophy, the metaphysical depth of Indian spiritual traditions, and the pragmatic realities of modern AI engineering. His reflections are not abstract speculations; they are grounded in lived spiritual practice, informed by decades in the technology sector, and tested against the real-world developments of generative AI systems that are transforming our daily lives.

At a moment when voices in neuroscience and philosophy claim consciousness to be an illusion, Consciousness and the AI Revolution offers a courageous counterpoint. It offers that consciousness is not a byproduct to be explained away, but the very ground upon which explanation itself stands. The book challenges us to see that our technological creations are not merely tools. They are mirrors reflecting back our deepest assumptions about what it means to be aware, to be self, to be alive.

In the pages that follow, you will not find dogma but dialogue between East and West, science and spirit, human and machine. That is the kind of conversation our world most needs, and Murugesan Narayanaswamy has given it to us with insight, humility, and grace.
